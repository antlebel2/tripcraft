# TripCraft Product Definition

## Problem

Many people want to travel but find planning a trip overwhelming, time-consuming, and anxiety-inducing. Travelers must research destinations, activities, accommodations, transportation, meals, reservations, and practical preparations across many disconnected sources.

Inexperienced travelers face an additional problem: they do not know what they do not know. A plan may look reasonable while containing unrealistic timing, difficult transportation, inconvenient locations, missing preparations, or outdated assumptions.

Existing resources can help with individual decisions, but they do not necessarily turn a traveler’s preferences and priorities into a complete, coherent, and realistic trip plan.

## Target Users

TripCraft is primarily for travelers who want help overcoming the complexity and uncertainty of trip planning, especially:

- Novice or infrequent travelers who lack planning experience.
- Travelers who find research and itinerary creation overwhelming.
- People who want reassurance that their plans are realistic and complete.
- Groups that need to coordinate decisions and maintain a shared plan.

TripCraft should remain useful to experienced travelers, but its primary purpose is to help people who would otherwise struggle with or postpone planning a trip.

## Product Vision

TripCraft removes the planning barrier between wanting to travel and feeling confident enough to go.

It meets travelers wherever they are in the planning process—from having only a desired type of experience, to knowing a destination, to having an itinerary that needs improvement. It guides them toward a personalized, realistic, and complete trip plan.

TripCraft is a structured travel-planning product that uses AI selectively where it provides meaningful value. AI supports the experience but is not the product itself.

## Value Proposition

TripCraft helps travelers turn their preferences, constraints, and must-do experiences into a realistic daily itinerary while guiding them through the other decisions required for a well-planned trip.

Unlike a general-purpose AI conversation, TripCraft maintains a persistent and structured plan. It connects recommendations, itinerary decisions, reservations, warnings, and unresolved planning tasks in one place.

Travelers remain in control while gaining confidence that:

- Their must-do experiences are included.
- Their daily plans are practical.
- Important planning decisions have not been overlooked.
- Recommendations reflect their preferences and logistical realities.
- Uncertain or potentially outdated information is clearly identified.

## Major Capabilities

TripCraft should eventually support the complete planning journey, including:

### Guided Trip Planning

- Begin with as little as a desired travel experience or destination.
- Learn the traveler’s dates, preferences, constraints, budget, and must-do experiences.
- Adapt its guidance to what the traveler already knows or has planned.
- Suggest destinations when the traveler has not yet selected one.

### Itinerary Creation and Improvement

- Turn preferences and must-dos into a realistic daily itinerary.
- Help users build an itinerary through guided decisions.
- Review an existing itinerary and identify problems or omissions.
- Recommend changes when timing, location, transportation, or other practical considerations make a plan unrealistic.
- Allow users to control how actively TripCraft offers guidance and warnings.

### Comprehensive Planning Support

Consider the major parts of a trip, including:

- Destination and dates.
- Budget.
- Activities and attractions.
- Daily itinerary.
- Transportation to and from the destination.
- Local transportation.
- Accommodations.
- Restaurants and meals.
- Reservations and tickets.
- Travel documents, entry requirements, packing, and other preparations.

TripCraft may explore and recommend options, but users complete purchases and bookings through specialized external services.

### Trip Readiness

- Track the traveler’s must-do experiences.
- Identify unresolved decisions and incomplete preparations.
- Track reservations or tickets that may still be needed.
- Highlight unverified assumptions and information requiring confirmation.
- Help users judge whether their trip is complete and ready.
- Allow users to decide when their plan is finished.

### Explainable Guidance

- Explain why a warning or recommendation is being made.
- Distinguish verified facts from estimates, assumptions, and user-provided information.
- Clearly warn users when information may be uncertain or outdated.
- Be willing to say when it does not know something.
- Encourage verification when confidence is limited.

### Collaboration

- Allow travelers to invite others to view or help plan a trip.
- Maintain a shared plan for the group.
- Support group input and, where useful, voting on planning choices.

### Organized Trip Information

- Store relevant details from arrangements made elsewhere, such as flight information, accommodation locations, and reservation details.
- Connect those details to the itinerary and overall plan without becoming the booking provider.

## Product Principles

### Realistic Before Impressive

An attractive itinerary is not useful if it cannot work in practice. TripCraft should prioritize feasibility, current information, and sound logistics over producing confident-looking plans.

### The Traveler Is the Final Decision-Maker

TripCraft proposes, explains, and warns. It does not silently change a traveler’s plans. Users approve all meaningful planning decisions.

### Meet Travelers Where They Are

A user may begin with only a vague idea, a selected destination, partial research, or a nearly complete itinerary. TripCraft should adapt rather than require everyone to follow the same starting process.

### Personalization Balanced With Practicality

Recommendations should reflect the traveler’s preferences and must-dos while accounting for time, geography, transportation, budget, and other practical constraints.

### Reduce Anxiety, Not Add to It

Guidance should make planning feel manageable. TripCraft should break complex planning into understandable decisions and make unresolved items visible without overwhelming the traveler.

### Be Honest About Uncertainty

TripCraft should clearly communicate what is known, estimated, assumed, outdated, or unverified. A transparent “I don’t know” is preferable to invented or misleading guidance.

### Maintain User Control

Travelers should be able to adjust the amount of guidance they receive. The product should support decision-making without taking ownership of the trip away from them.

### Use AI Purposefully

AI should be used where it materially improves planning, personalization, or guidance. Core product value should not depend on presenting TripCraft as a general-purpose AI chatbot.

## What TripCraft Is Not

TripCraft is not:

- A flight, accommodation, restaurant, activity, or ticket booking service.
- A replacement for specialized booking products.
- A travel agency.
- A travel social network.
- A public review platform.
- An expense-tracking or financial-management product.
- A photo journal or travel diary.
- A system for managing travel providers.
- A general-purpose conversational assistant that produces disposable itineraries.

TripCraft may store details from external bookings and recommend external options, but it does not own or complete those transactions.

## Initial Product Focus

The first version should focus on one primary outcome:

> Turn a traveler’s preferences, constraints, dates, and must-do experiences into a realistic daily itinerary.

This establishes TripCraft’s central value while leaving broader trip-readiness guidance, collaboration, destination discovery, and deeper planning support available for later development and prioritization.

## Future Vision

Over time, TripCraft can become a comprehensive planning companion that:

- Supports the full planning journey from destination inspiration to trip readiness.
- Provides increasingly personalized recommendations as it learns a traveler’s tastes with their permission.
- Reviews and improves plans with clear, explainable guidance.
- Helps groups coordinate decisions and maintain a shared source of truth.
- Organizes externally booked travel details alongside the itinerary.
- Gives travelers confidence that their plans are complete, realistic, and suited to them.

The long-term goal is not to automate travelers out of planning. It is to remove the overwhelming work and uncertainty that prevent people from turning travel ideas into trips they feel ready to take.
